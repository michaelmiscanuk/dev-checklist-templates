# Running a one-on-one with a report

When: Before a weekly or biweekly 1:1, or when your 1:1s have turned into status updates nobody gets anything from.

How to use: Open this before each 1:1 and keep it beside the shared doc while you meet. Each check says what to do; the line under it is a tip for doing it well. 'How to verify' says what to look at to know it worked. The first moment is for setting up or resetting your 1:1s; the rest come round every time.

Before you start: Which of these fits this report? (any that apply) New to me, first months / Was my peer before / None of these. Checks marked "only if" apply only to that answer.

## 1. When you set up the 1:1s

You are starting 1:1s with a report, or resetting ones that slid into status updates.

Must:
- [ ] Agree the meeting is theirs
  Why: Say it plainly: **their topics go first**, and project status has another home.
  How to verify: Ask the report what this meeting is for. They describe time for their concerns, not a report to you.
- [ ] Book a recurring protected slot
  Why: Pick a time you rarely need to move, and treat it like a meeting with a customer.
  How to verify: Look at both calendars: the series repeats with no end date and nothing clashes in the next month.

Don't forget these too:
- [ ] Choose weekly or every two weeks
  Why: Start weekly with someone new or in a big change; move to every two weeks only when things are steady.
  How to verify: The cadence fits where the report is right now, and they agreed to it rather than just accepted it.
- [ ] Set up one shared running doc
  Why: Put the newest meeting at the top so both of you add topics during the week and see past actions.
  How to verify: Open the doc: you can both edit it, and it holds dated sections with actions.
- [ ] Give status updates another home (source 1)
  Why: Move project status to standups, tickets or a short written note, so the meeting keeps room for people topics.
  How to verify: You have named where **status updates go** instead, and the report knows where to find them.
- [ ] Ask how they like feedback (source 2) (only if: New to me, first months)
  Why: Ask in the first 1:1: written or spoken, in the moment or saved up, praise in public or in private.
  How to verify: Their answer is written in the doc and you can say it back without looking.
- [ ] Learn their goals and working style (only if: New to me, first months)
  Why: Ask what their best manager did, what drains their energy, and where they want to grow.
  How to verify: The doc holds their answers in their own words.
- [ ] Name the change in roles (only if: Was my peer before)
  Why: Say what stays the same, what changes, and that you will be **fair to everyone** on the team.
  How to verify: You have both said out loud **what stays and what changes**, and the report has answered.
- [ ] Keep manager-only matters private (only if: Was my peer before)
  Why: Venting together about the team or leadership ends now; say so kindly before it comes up.
  How to verify: In recent talks, **you shared nothing** about other people's pay, performance or private matters.
- [ ] Say where confidentiality ends
  Why: Tell them early that most things stay between you, but **harassment, safety or legal matters** are ones you must act on.
  How to verify: When they ask 'can I tell you something in confidence?', you promise discretion, never secrecy, before hearing it.

Move on when A recurring slot is on both calendars and the report has seen the shared doc.

## 2. Before each 1:1 (every time)

A day or an hour before the meeting, with the shared doc open.

Must:
- [ ] Hold the slot, never cancel
  Why: Move it within the same week if you must; only sickness, leave or a real emergency cancels it.
  How to verify: Look back over the last two months: every meeting happened or moved, **none simply dropped**.
- [ ] Read last time's notes first
  How to verify: You can name each open action, who owns it and whether it is done.

Don't forget these too:
- [ ] Ask them to add topics
  Why: A short reminder the day before gets topics written down, not remembered on the spot.
  How to verify: Their topics sit in the doc before the meeting starts, above yours.
- [ ] Note one specific thing to recognize
  Why: Skim recent work, reviews or messages for something they find meaningful; **specific praise lands**, generic praise fades.
  How to verify: You can name what they did, when, and the effect it had.
- [ ] Bring context they lack
  Why: Pick what changed above the team that touches their work: priorities, decisions, plans.
  How to verify: Your items in the doc are few, and each is something only you could tell them.
- [ ] Prepare feedback that cannot wait
  Why: Use **Situation-Behavior-Impact (SBI)**: the situation, what they did and its impact, never their character.
  How to verify: Your note names a recent moment and a behavior, with no words like attitude or lazy.

Stop if:
- If the slot keeps slipping: stop and reset it with them

Move on when the doc shows their topics, your few items and last time's open actions.

## 3. During the 1:1 (every time)

In the room or on the call, with the shared doc open between you.

Must:
- [ ] Start with their topics
  Why: Open with 'What is on your mind?' and wait before adding anything of yours.
  How to verify: Their items were the first ones discussed today.
- [ ] Let them do most talking
  Why: Count to four after each answer: people often add **the real point** once the silence sits.
  How to verify: Looking back, they spoke most of the time and your notes are mostly their words.
- [ ] Agree actions with owners
  Why: Read them back in the last few minutes so you both leave with the same list.
  How to verify: Every action in today's section has **a name and a date**.

Don't forget these too:
- [ ] Close everything but the doc
  Why: Keep only the shared doc on screen and your phone face down.
  How to verify: Nothing pinged or pulled your eyes away during the meeting.
- [ ] Ask what and how questions
  Why: Start questions with what or how; why can sound like blame.
  How to verify: Most of your questions were **open-ended**, not ones they could answer with yes or no.
- [ ] Coach before you solve
  Why: Ask what they have tried and what they would do; give your answer only when they are stuck.
  How to verify: On problems they raised, they proposed the next step most of the time.
- [ ] Ask how they are doing
  Why: Ask about energy and workload, not only tasks; a score out of ten makes an honest answer easier.
  How to verify: You know how stretched they are, and what is causing it.
- [ ] Ask what is blocking them
  Why: Ask what slows them down that you could remove: an approval, a decision, another team.
  How to verify: Each blocker named today has an owner in the doc, often you.
- [ ] Give the feedback you prepared
  Why: After you describe the impact, **ask for their view**; it turns a verdict into a conversation.
  How to verify: They said back what they heard and what they will try next.
- [ ] Ask for feedback on yourself
  Why: Ask something specific, like 'What should I change about planning?'; general asks get polite answers.
  How to verify: At least once a month, the doc records something they asked you to change.
- [ ] Ask what they need from you
  Why: Before you answer a problem, ask whether they want **help, a decision** or just to be heard.
  How to verify: For each problem they raised today, you can say which of the three they wanted.
- [ ] Thank feedback, never defend (source 3)
  Why: Say thanks, ask for an example and take notes; respond later, after reflecting, if at all.
  How to verify: In later meetings they still name things you could do differently, **without being pushed**.

Stop if:
- If they report harassment, discrimination or a safety risk: tell them you must act, then call HR
- If they mention self-harm or a crisis: drop the agenda, stay with them, point to professional help

Move on when each topic ended with an action or a clear 'nothing needed', and you both know the next step.

## 4. Right after the 1:1 (every time)

The meeting has just ended and the details are still fresh.

Must:
- [ ] Do what you promised
  Why: Put each of your actions on your own task list straight away, dated before the next meeting.
  How to verify: At the next meeting, every action you owned is done, or you explain why before they ask.

Don't forget these too:
- [ ] Finish notes the same day
  Why: Write decisions and actions in a few lines each while you still remember them.
  How to verify: Today's section holds the topics, decisions and actions, short enough to read in a minute.
- [ ] Keep sensitive details private
  Why: Health, family or worries about a colleague go in your own notes only, written as **facts you would show them**.
  How to verify: The shared doc holds nothing told in confidence, and your own notes hold facts, not judgments.

Move on when today's notes are in the doc and your own actions are on your task list.

## 5. Once a month or quarter (every time)

Step back from the weekly rhythm and look at the pattern across meetings.

Must:
- [ ] Hold a separate career conversation
  Why: Book it **apart from weekly meetings** so urgent work cannot crowd it out.
  How to verify: Each quarter the doc has a career section with a goal and one step toward it.

Don't forget these too:
- [ ] Ask how the meetings work
  Why: Ask what to keep, drop or change, then change one thing so they see the answer matters.
  How to verify: The format or cadence has changed at least once because of what they said.
- [ ] Look for themes across weeks
  Why: Scan past notes for topics that keep coming back: the same blocker, frustration or wish.
  How to verify: You can name the two or three recurring themes and what you are doing about each.

Stop if:
- If their work misses the bar week after week: stop coaching here and open the underperformance checklist

Move on when A career talk is done or booked this quarter, and you asked how meetings work for them.

Not covered: Ongoing underperformance and formal reviews have their own entries. Skip-levels, team meetings and conflict between two engineers are left out too.
